Where the forest runs into the sea

The Margaret River region is a narrow ridge of limestone and granite at the south-west tip of Australia. On one side, Indian Ocean swell. On the other, vineyards, karri forest and more than a hundred caves.

Chapter one

Swell that has crossed an ocean

Storms deep in the Southern Ocean send long-period swell north-east until it meets the reefs off Prevelly and Yallingup. Surfers come for the power. Everyone else comes for the colour of the water, the granite headlands and the quiet bays sheltered from the westerly.

  • Surfers PointPrevelly

    The main break, home to the region's international surf contest each autumn. Watch from the lookout at the river mouth.

  • Hamelin BaySouth coast

    Old jetty pylons, calm water and wild stingrays that cruise the shallows near the boat ramp.

  • Canal RocksYallingup

    A chain of split granite islands joined by a footbridge, with the sea surging through the gaps.

  • Meelup BeachGeographe Bay

    North-facing and sheltered. The swimming beach for families on a windy day.

Chapter two

A maritime climate in every glass

In 1965 an agronomist compared Margaret River's weather with Bordeaux and found it close. The first vines went into the gravel loam at Wilyabrup two years later. The ocean keeps summers mild and winters wet, which gives slow ripening and wines with structure.

  • Cabernet SauvignonRed

    The signature red. Firm, cedar and blackcurrant, built to age.

  • ChardonnayWhite

    Rich but tight, with natural acidity from the sea breeze.

  • Semillon Sauvignon BlancBlend

    The local white blend. Crisp, grassy and made for oysters.

Chapter three

Karri, pale and impossibly tall

Boranup Forest grows on a thin strip of soil just behind the dunes. The karri here are regrowth after logging a century ago, and they already reach past sixty metres. Their bark sheds in summer, leaving smooth trunks in grey, cream and salmon.

  • Boranup Drive14 km gravel

    A slow, unsealed road through the middle of the forest, with a lookout to the coast.

  • EllensbrookWalk trail

    A short walk from a homestead built in 1857 to Meekadarabee Falls, a cave behind a curtain of water.

  • Cape to Cape Track5 to 8 days

    The full walk follows cliffs, beaches and forest between the two lighthouses.

Chapter four

A ridge of limestone, hollowed out

Rain filtering through the Leeuwin-Naturaliste Ridge has carved over a hundred caves. They hold a steady temperature all year, so they make a good plan for a wet winter day.

  • Jewel CaveNear Augusta

    The largest show cave in Western Australia, with one of the longest straw stalactites found in a tourist cave.

  • Lake CaveBoranup

    Reached down a deep collapsed doline. A still lake reflects a suspended table formation.

  • Mammoth CaveSelf-guided

    Fossils of extinct megafauna were found in its floor.

  • Ngilgi CaveYallingup

    Named in a Wadandi story of a battle between a good and an evil spirit.

The lay of the land

Between two lighthouses

The whole region fits in a strip about 100 km long and rarely more than 30 km wide. Cape Naturaliste looks north over Geographe Bay. Cape Leeuwin, at the bottom, is where the Indian and Southern Oceans meet.

When to come

Six seasons, not four

The Wadandi people, the saltwater people of this coast, read the year in six seasons tied to weather, plants and animals. Pick one to see what the region is doing.
